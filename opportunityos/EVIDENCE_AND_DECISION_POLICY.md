# OpportunityOS Evidence & Decision Policy

## Why this exists
Four independent reviews agreed that the strongest part of OpportunityOS is the Macro → Micro → Action framework, but warned that evidence quality, time-sensitive claims, subjective entry-stage labels and over-automation could damage trust. This policy converts those criticisms into product rules.

## 1. Every important claim must be one of three things

### FACT
A claim directly supported by a source. For live opportunities, prefer the official notice, regulator, statistical agency or primary issuer.

### INTERPRETATION
Our analysis of what the fact may mean. It must never be presented as confirmed fact.

### BUSINESS OPPORTUNITY
A proposed commercial action. It must show assumptions, uncertainties and the next validation step.

## 2. Evidence grades
- **A** — Official / primary source directly supports the claim.
- **B** — High-quality secondary source, ideally corroborated by another reliable source.
- **C** — Demo, single-source, stale or incompletely verified information.
- **D** — Speculative signal, anecdote or weak source. Never actionable without validation.

## 3. Confidence
Every opportunity carries a 0–100 evidence-confidence score. Confidence is not the same as company fit.

The public Decision Score is:

`Decision Score = 70% Company Fit + 30% Evidence Confidence`

A high fit with weak evidence must not become an APPLY recommendation.

## 4. Entry-stage labels use explicit rules
- **ATTRACTIVE** — Decision Score ≥85 and confidence ≥80; strong evidence and strong company fit.
- **SELECTIVE** — Decision Score ≥70 with remaining execution or verification risk.
- **EARLY** — promising signal but insufficient maturity or evidence.
- **VALIDATE FIRST** — demo data or confidence <60.
- **AVOID / TOO WEAK** — Decision Score <50, ineligible, closed, or uneconomic.

These are business-validation labels, not investment advice.

## 5. Time-sensitive facts
Oil, FX, freight, geopolitics, regulation, drug approvals, tenders and market prices must carry a last-checked timestamp. Stale data must lose confidence or be withheld.

## 6. Source hierarchy
1. Official tender / regulator / statistical agency / company filing
2. Primary company statement
3. Reuters / AP / FT / BBC or equivalent high-quality reporting
4. Specialist trade source
5. Other secondary source

Major claims should be corroborated when practical. Very large numbers or exceptional claims require primary-source confirmation before being presented as verified.

## 7. Automation boundary
Do not pretend the MVP can fully automate eligibility, win probability or ROI. Start with deterministic rules and explicit unknowns. Use AI to extract and explain ambiguous text; require human verification for high-impact actions.

## 8. Commercial validation before product expansion
Manual first, then automate repeated work. Initial validation products:
- £29 Opportunity Intelligence Report
- £49 AI Security Check
- £500–£1,000 implementation / workflow automation where customer demand is proven

Do not build Food/Import/Critical Mineral products as separate apps until paid demand exists.

## 9. Market focus
Phase 1: UK SMEs, British English, UK opportunities.
Phase 2: UK↔Korea cross-border opportunities.
Phase 3: EU and selected enterprise intelligence.

UK-first is a launch strategy, not a claim that the product is globally complete.

## 10. What creates defensibility
Not generic information. The moat is:
- Company DNA
- Normalised opportunity data
- Eligibility rules
- Evidence history
- User actions
- Win/loss outcomes
- Supplier/partner graph

The long-term product is a Decision Engine, not a search engine.
